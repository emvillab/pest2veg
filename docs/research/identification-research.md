# Pest Tech – Identification Research

## Research Purpose

The purpose of this research was to investigate how Pest Tech could help UK farmers, vegetable growers and allotment holders identify possible crop pests using information such as the affected crop, visible symptoms or damage, and the location of the damage.

## Source 1 — Garden Organic: Pests and Diseases

### What I learned
I learned that Garden Organic focuses heavily on preventing pest problems rather than simply treating them afterwards. It recommends approaches such as healthy soil, crop planning and rotation, encouraging beneficial insects and using physical barriers instead of relying heavily on chemical treatments.

### How it could help Pest Tech
This could help us structure the information shown after a user identifies a possible pest. Alongside identification and damage symptoms, Pest Tech could provide responsible prevention and control advice, particularly methods suitable for UK farmers, vegetable growers and allotment holders.

### Limitation
The page does not provide enough detailed symptom-to-pest relationships to build our identification feature by itself. More specific sources will be needed for this.

### Source
Garden Organic — Pests and Diseases  
https://www.gardenorganic.org.uk/expert-advice/garden-management/pests-and-diseases

## Source 2 — AHDB: Sources, spread and survival of pathogens, pests and weeds in ornamental crops

### What I learned
I learned that pests can spread and survive in several different ways, including through plants, soil, equipment, water and surrounding vegetation. The source also showed that different pests survive for different lengths of time and that good hygiene, monitoring and prevention can reduce the risk of pests spreading.

### How it could help Pest Tech
This source could help Pest Tech provide useful prevention information alongside the pest identification results. It also gave me the idea that the location of damage, such as leaves, roots or stems, could potentially be another factor used to narrow down possible pest matches.

### Limitation
The source focuses on ornamental crops rather than vegetables, so it would not be suitable as the main source for creating our crop-to-pest identification data. More specific sources will be needed for common UK vegetable pests and their symptoms.

### Source
AHDB — Sources, spread and survival of pathogens, pests and weeds in ornamental crops  
https://horticulture.ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/sources-spread-survival-pathogens-pests-weeds-ornamentals

## Source 3 — RHS: 2020's top garden pests and diseases revealed

### What I learned
I learned that slugs and snails were the most commonly reported garden pests to the RHS in 2020, with crops such as potatoes and beans being affected. Other commonly reported pests included vine weevils, aphids, spider mites and thrips. The source also showed that the prevalence of different pests can change from year to year.

### How it could help Pest Tech
This source could help us decide which common UK pests should be prioritised within Pest Tech. It also provides some useful links between pests and affected crops, which could contribute towards our crop → symptoms/damage → possible pest matches identification process.

### Limitation
The information is based on enquiries received by the RHS during 2020, so it should not be treated as a current ranking of the most common UK pests. The article also covers garden diseases and ornamental plants as well as crop pests, and does not provide enough detailed symptom-to-pest information to build our identification feature on its own.

### Source
RHS — 2020's top garden pests and diseases revealed  
https://www.rhs.org.uk/science/articles/2020-pests-and-diseases

## Source 4 — Biopredator: Pest Identification Tool

### What I learned
I learned that an online pest identification tool can guide users through a series of questions about the problem they are experiencing before suggesting possible pests. This showed me how an identification process can be broken down into simple stages rather than expecting the user to already know which pest they are looking for.

### How it could help Pest Tech
Biopredator provides a useful example for the interactive identification feature we are proposing for Pest Tech. Our version could use a simpler process where the user selects the affected crop, visible symptoms or damage, and optionally the location of the damage before being shown possible pest matches.

### Limitation
The identification process is more detailed than what we are currently proposing for Pest Tech. Trying to reproduce a complex identification system could make the project too large for the available development time, so it should be used as inspiration rather than something to copy directly.

### Source
Biopredator — Pest Identification Tool  
https://www.biopredator.co.uk/pest-id

## Research Conclusion

The research suggests that Pest Tech should use a simple identification process rather than attempting to provide a definite diagnosis. My proposed process is:

**Select crop → Select symptoms/damage → Select damage location (optional) → View possible pest matches → View pest details**

The research also showed that prevention and responsible pest management should be included alongside identification information. More detailed research will be needed to accurately link individual UK crops, symptoms and pests before this information is added to the website.

This proposed identification process will be discussed with the group before development begins.
