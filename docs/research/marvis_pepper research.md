# Pests of UK Pepper

UK pepper is mostly sweet pepper grown in heated glasshouses. These entries cover the pests that affect UK pepper growers, with evidence from AHDB, RHS, Defra, ADAS, Rothamsted and a Harper Adams University thesis. Entries marked "international data" use basic biology from non-UK extension sheets. No pesticide products are named, so check current UK approvals before using any.

## Peach-potato aphid (green peach aphid) {#peach-potato-aphid}

**Scientific name:** *Myzus persicae*  
**Crop:** Pepper

### Summary

The most common aphid pest of UK sweet pepper. A soft-bodied sap feeder, 1.2 to 2.3 mm long. In the UK it reproduces without mating, so it can breed all year under glass. At 20°C it reaches reproducing adulthood in about 7.4 days. Other aphids on pepper include the potato aphid, the glasshouse-potato (foxglove) aphid and the melon-cotton aphid.

### Damage and symptoms

Distorted, stunted growth, and honeydew with sooty mould on leaves and fruit. Feeding on young fruit buds leaves white spots on the fruit. Aphids spread plant viruses, and seedlings suffer most. Fruit with aphids, aphid skins or sooty mould fails the UNECE marketing standard. Yield loss, control and washing costs average over £100,000 per hectare per season (a figure from Jacobson, 2010, quoted in Shaw, 2023).

### Location

Soft shoot tips and leaf undersides in UK glasshouses. Check seedlings and incoming transplants, because aphids can arrive on young plants. Swollen bronze or black mummies show that parasitic wasps are at work.

### Prevention and management

Weekly preventive releases of the wasp Aphidius colemani early in the year (at least 0.15 per m²), raised to 0.5 to 1 per m² a week for at least three weeks once traps catch aphids. Check transplants on arrival, remove dead plants and steam-clean between crops. UK peach-potato aphids show strong pirimicarb and pyrethroid resistance, so sprays are unreliable.

## Western flower thrips {#western-flower-thrips}

**Scientific name:** *Frankliniella occidentalis*  
**Crop:** Pepper

### Summary

The main thrips damaging UK protected crops, and resistant to many plant protection products. Adults are slender, about 1 to 1.5 mm, light yellow to brown, with fringed wings. Egg to adult takes 7 to 14 days at 20 to 37°C (international data). Onion thrips is also listed on UK pepper.

### Damage and symptoms

Silvery patches with black specks on leaves, distorted growth and deformed flowers. Thrips spread tomato spotted wilt virus.

### Location

Flowers, folds of buds and new foliage. Larvae shelter inside flowers and developing tips. Pupae sit in the soil or growing media. Adults are hard to find, so use sticky cards and inspect incoming plants.

### Prevention and management

Start biological control before thrips or damage appear. The UK-native Orius bug is widely used in protected sweet pepper, and predatory mites help. Keep weeds and debris out of and around the growing area and inspect incoming plants. Use a chemical only if necessary, and choose the option safest for the predators.

## Red spider mite (two-spotted spider mite) {#red-spider-mite}

**Scientific name:** *Tetranychus urticae*  
**Crop:** Pepper

### Summary

A sap-feeding mite about 0.4 mm long. It thrives in hot, dry conditions and is hard to control under glass because of chemical resistance. Egg to adult takes 7 to 21 days (international data). It can be confused with Lewis spider mite, which needs laboratory examination to tell apart.

### Damage and symptoms

Mottled, pale leaves with fine webbing, and early leaf drop.

### Location

Undersides of older leaves, along the veins. Look for cast skins, empty eggshells and webbing with a hand lens. In UK glasshouses the mite overwinters in cracks and crevices.

### Prevention and management

Clean the glasshouse in winter and raise humidity by damping down. Release the predatory mite Phytoseiulus persimilis, the most common biological control. Amblyseius californicus works as a preventative and survives winter better.

## Glasshouse whitefly {#glasshouse-whitefly}

**Scientific name:** *Trialeurodes vaporariorum*  
**Crop:** Pepper

### Summary

A white sap-feeding fly. Adults fly up when disturbed, and nymphs are scale-like. A female lays over 200 eggs. The cycle takes about three weeks at 21°C but several months at 10°C. There are many generations a year, and it persists under glass on leftover plant material, including weeds.

### Damage and symptoms

Adults and nymphs feed on sap and weaken plants. Honeydew leads to sooty mould, which spoils fruit appearance.

### Location

Undersides of leaves. Shake the plant and watch for white flies rising. Parasitised nymphs turn black, which shows the wasps are working.

### Prevention and management

Quarantine new plants and remove unwanted plants and weeds. Release the parasitic wasp Encarsia formosa and use sticky traps for monitoring. Amblyseius swirskii predatory mites also feed on whitefly.

## Tomato moth (moth caterpillars) {#tomato-moth}

**Scientific name:** *Lacanobia oleracea (cabbage moth, Mamestra brassicae, also attacks sweet pepper)*  
**Crop:** Pepper

### Summary

An occasional pest of glasshouse crops. Females lay round eggs in groups of 50 to 300 under leaves. Caterpillars pupate just below the soil surface or under rockwool matting. Evidence for pepper specifically is thin and comes mainly from supplier pages.

### Damage and symptoms

Large caterpillars eat big areas of leaf and can attack stems. Frass on fruit lowers marketability, and caterpillars eat large holes in green and ripening fruit. The second generation often causes more fruit loss than the first.

### Location

Undersides of leaves for egg groups, and leaves and fruit for feeding. Pupae sit just below the soil surface or under rockwool matting.

### Prevention and management

Check regularly for leaf damage and remove caterpillars early. UK glasshouse research has tested Bacillus thuringiensis and the wasp Eulophus pennicornis against tomato moth on tomatoes. Suppliers list biological options for sweet pepper.

## Hyperparasitoid wasps {#hyperparasitoid-wasps}

**Scientific name:** *Asaphes suspensus and Dendrocerus species*  
**Crop:** Pepper

### Summary

Small wasps that lay eggs on the beneficial wasp grub inside an aphid mummy, so they attack the wasps used to control aphids. Seven species were found in UK pepper crops in 2010. Asaphes suspensus takes about 18 days from egg to adult at 20°C, longer than the 13 days of Aphidius wasps.

### Damage and symptoms

No direct harm to pepper. They kill the beneficial wasps that keep aphids down, which can make aphid control fail. ADAS calls them a major threat to biological control of aphids in UK crops.

### Location

Aphid mummies on leaves, and the wasps that emerge from them. Mummies from Aphidius are bronze and from Aphelinus are black. Ask your supplier or adviser to identify wasps from a sample.

### Prevention and management

No proven prevention tool yet. The usual response is to stop releasing parasitic wasps, use a broad-spectrum insecticide, then release the aphid midge Aphidoletes. This costs more and kills the beneficial wasps too. Research aims to use scent-based traps to lure hyperparasitoids away.

- [Jacobson, Hyperparasitoids: a threat to IPM of aphids on sweet pepper? (IOBC/WPRS)](https://iobc-wprs.org/product/hyperparasitoids-a-threat-to-ipm-of-aphids-on-sweet-pepper/)

## Fungus gnats (sciarid flies) {#fungus-gnats}

**Scientific name:** *Family Sciaridae (often Bradysia species)*  
**Crop:** Pepper

### Summary

Dark flies 3 to 5 mm long whose larvae live in damp growing media. Females lay 50 to 200 eggs, and egg to adult takes 3 to 4 weeks in warm conditions. They breed all year in greenhouses. Mainly a problem for seedlings and young plants.

### Damage and symptoms

Larvae feed mainly on dead roots and decaying material. Some damage seedling roots and soft stem bases, and wounds can let in root diseases.

### Location

The top inch of the growing media near the roots. Adults sit in warm, humid spots near plants. Check seedlings and young plants first.

### Prevention and management

Avoid overwatering. Repot into fresh compost and discard the old. Add nematodes or predators to the compost. The soil mite Stratiolaelaps scimitus eats gnat larvae and thrips pupae.
